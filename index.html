AI4AI-Bench: Auditing Recursive Self-Improvement in LLM Agents

Anonymous Author(s) · Paper preview · Download PDF

Abstract. Large language model agents are increasingly capable of modifying their own runtime harnesses, raising a critical question: how do we know when self-improvement is genuine versus overfitting or degenerative? We introduce SIAS (Self-Improvement Audit Score), a quantitative framework that disentangles true generalization gains from training-set overfitting and destructive regression. Auditing the 27 published harness patches from the Harness-R1 held-out generalization study (1,270 tasks, three seeds), we find two frontier editors are net-negative out-of-batch (Qwen3.5-397B −4.33pp, DeepSeek-V4-Pro −0.42pp) with a 70% destruction rate concentrated in identifiable patches, while a subset-audit gate with a 50-task budget reaches 87% decision accuracy and recovers +251 net tasks (+19.8pp) relative to indiscriminate acceptance.
Data honesty note. All headline numbers derive from real records publicly released by the Harness-R1 authors (Apache 2.0). Simulation results are explicitly labeled as such.

The SIAS Metric

SIAS = α·Δholdout − β·max(0, Δtrain − Δholdout) − γ·C(p)

Defaults α=1.0, β=0.3, γ=0.0005. Six degradation patterns detected automatically: MyopicFix, OverGeneralization, ContextBloat, RewardHacking, NegativeTransfer, PromptDrift.

Data Source
